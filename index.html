How this works

The chart. Thirty indicators of democratic health, scored 0–100. Press ▶ or drag the year slider: 1975–2020 replay measured U.S. values (GSoD Indices v5.1, ×100), 2021–2025 bridge to today illustratively, and muted columns have no pre-2023 data. The dotted mark on a column is its measured 2020 value; a short red line under ▼ marks the old 1975–2020 floor the 2026 report says that indicator has now fallen below, and a gray line appears where some past year dipped lower than today’s score; ▼ marks an indicator the 2026 report puts at a 50-year low. Hover any column for detail; click it for its full 45-year line.

The levers. Each bundles the choices a society makes in one arena (0 = weaker, 100 = stronger); every indicator listens to two or three of them, weighted by how strongly that arena shapes it. Each lever starts where that arena stands today — unevenly, as the report describes — and the tick under the slider marks that starting point.

  • Electoral integrity & access — election administration, voting access, fair competition between parties.
  • Press freedom & expression — safety of journalists, independent media, room for critical speech.
  • Judicial independence — courts free of political pressure; rulings respected and enforced.
  • Executive restraint — self-limits on power: no punishing critics, respect for constraints.
  • Transparency & anti-corruption — open books, sanctioned misconduct, merit-based public service.
  • Civic engagement & cohesion — participation, cross-party cooperation, tolerance for the other side.

About this prototype

Why it exists. A conversation starter for the reunion topic “thirty indicators of a healthy civilization / democracy,” prompted by NPR’s September 15, 2026 story, “State of U.S. democracy reaches new low, report finds.”

The report behind it. That story covers The Global State of Democracy 2026: Democracy in an Age of Conflict, from the Stockholm-based International Institute for Democracy and Electoral Assistance, whose indices track 30 aspects of democracy across 174 countries back to 1975. Its U.S. findings: nearly half of the thirty indicators fell to their lowest levels in the dataset’s fifty-year history, and seven declined significantly between 2020 and 2025 — access to justice, economic equality, freedom of expression, freedom of the press, effective parliament, judicial independence, and free political parties. All but the last are at record lows (marked ▼ on the chart). Globally, 2025 was the eleventh straight year in which more countries declined than advanced, and rule of law was the weakest-performing category, with 71 countries scoring low.

What is and isn’t real. Real: the indicator framework, the ▼ flags, the global statistics above, and the dotted marks on the chart — those are the United States’ measured scores for 2020 from IDEA’s official GSoD Indices dataset (v5.1, the newest vintage with per-indicator values available to this page; scaled ×100). The gap between a dotted mark and today’s column is the reported 2020–2025 decline, sized illustratively. Illustrative: today’s 0–100 starting scores and the lever weights. IDEA’s v9 dataset (through 2024) is freely downloadable and can replace the 2020 marks when available. A natural committee exercise: argue about the weights — that argument is the discussion of what holds a democracy up.
